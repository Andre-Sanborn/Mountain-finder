# The home session: 15 minutes with the iPhone

This is the first time a person holds the phone up to the app. It takes about fifteen
minutes and it happens at home, outdoors, in the sun. Nothing is uploaded. At the end you
email one small file to yourself, and tell Claude in the chat that you sent it.

What the session is for, in one sentence: the phone's compass and tilt sensors have never
been checked against a known direction, and the sun is the only known direction you have at
home. The rest of the project waits on that check.

---

## Before you start

### Check the page is live

Open this address in Safari on any device:

```
https://andre-sanborn.github.io/Mountain-finder/live.html
```

If a page appears with the title **Mountain Finder — live**, the site is published and you
can carry on. If you get a "404" or "There isn't a GitHub Pages site here", GitHub Pages is
not switched on yet. Stop, and tell Claude. The session cannot run until it is on.

### Pick the time of day

You need the sun **between 15° and 50° above the horizon**, and you need to be able to see
it. Mid-morning or mid-afternoon on a clear day. Not noon, and not near sunset.

How to tell without a tool:

- Stretch one arm out straight and make a fist. A fist at arm's length covers about 10° of
  sky.
- Stack fists from the horizon up to the sun. **One and a half fists to five fists** is the
  range you want.
- Do not look straight at the sun to do this. Look at the sky beside it.

A second check: on flat ground, if your shadow is longer than you are tall, the sun is below
45°. That is the top half of the range.

The app also checks for you. If the sun is too low or too high, it says so on screen and
tells you to come back at a different hour.

### Have these ready

- **The iPhone, charged above 50%.** The screen, camera and GPS all run for the whole
  session.
- **Somewhere to lean the phone sideways for three minutes.** One step asks you to put the
  phone down against something and not touch it. A wall, a step, a plant pot, a bag.
- **A spot outdoors where you can see the sun**, with room to turn all the way round and to
  walk twenty paces in a straight line.
- **Wi-Fi at that spot, or just before you go out.** One step downloads about 40 MB.

## Step 1 — Open the page in Safari

Open the **Safari** app on the iPhone. Tap the address bar at the bottom, and type or paste
this exactly:

```
https://andre-sanborn.github.io/Mountain-finder/live.html?session=home
```

Then tap **Go**.

Open it as a normal Safari tab, not from an icon saved to the Home Screen: a Home Screen
copy gets its own separate permissions and its own separate storage, so the camera you allow
and the terrain you download in one of them does not count for the other.

**Turn the phone sideways**, long edge level, the way you would hold it to film something.
The app only draws in landscape. If it stays portrait, swipe down from the top-right corner
of the screen and check the padlock-with-an-arrow button (Rotation Lock) is off.

## Step 2 — Let the phone use its camera, sensors and location

Tap the **Start** button at the top of the page.

The phone now asks three questions, one after another. Each is a small grey box in the
middle of the screen. Tap the left-hand or bottom option on each:

1. **"andre-sanborn.github.io" Would Like to Access the Camera** — tap **Allow**.
2. **"andre-sanborn.github.io" Would Like to Access Motion and Orientation** — tap
   **Allow**.
3. **"andre-sanborn.github.io" Would Like To Use Your Current Location** — tap **Allow While
   Using App**. ("Allow Once" also works, but then the phone asks again after every reload.)

The exact wording of these boxes varies a little between iOS versions. The pattern is the
same: the address of the site, then what it wants.

You should now see the camera picture filling the screen, with a strip of text at the top
and another at the bottom.

## Step 3 — Download the terrain for offline use

Scroll the bottom strip up with a finger. Near the bottom is a button that reads something
like:

**Download Bogus Basin 60 km for offline use (42.5 MB)**

The name and the number depend on where you are standing. Tap it. It takes a minute or two
on Wi-Fi. A line underneath counts the progress, and then says how many files were kept.

Do this **before** turning airplane mode on. With no signal there is nothing to download.

If instead it says "This deployment serves no terrain here, so there is nothing to keep",
the published site does not carry terrain for your part of the world. Write that sentence
down for Claude and carry on with the rest of the session; only the offline check is lost.

## Step 4 — Prove it works with no signal

1. Swipe **down from the top-right corner** of the screen. The Control Centre panel appears.
2. Tap the **aeroplane** button. It turns orange. Airplane mode is on.
3. Swipe up from the bottom to close the panel.
4. Tap the address bar, then tap the **circular arrow** (reload) at its right-hand end.

The page should load again and draw labels, with no signal at all. GPS still works in
airplane mode, so your location is still found; it may take a few seconds longer than usual.

The phone may ask the three permission questions again after the reload. Tap **Allow** on
each. Whether they survive a reload is one of the things this session is finding out, so
note what happened.

Leave airplane mode on for the rest of the session.

## Step 5 — Start the recording

Tap **Start** again at the top if the page asks you to, and allow the three questions again
if they appear.

A panel headed **Home session** is at the top of the screen. It first lists what goes into
the file and what does not. Read it. In short: sensor readings and camera frame sizes go in;
your location, the time and any picture do not.

Tap **Start recording**.

If the button is greyed out, one of two lines under it says why: either you have not tapped
Start and allowed the three questions yet, or the phone is still finding your location.

## Step 6 — The fourteen steps on screen

Each step shows one instruction and a countdown in seconds. When the countdown runs out the
app moves on by itself. A **Move on now** button skips ahead when you have finished early.

These are the fourteen, in order, with the words the screen uses:

1. **Flat, screen up** — Put the phone flat on a table with the screen facing the ceiling.
   (4 seconds)
2. **Upright** — Pick the phone up and hold it upright, tall way up, screen facing you.
   (4 seconds)
3. **Flat, screen down** — Put the phone face down on the table, so the camera points at the
   ceiling. (4 seconds)
4. **Right edge down** — Hold the phone upright again, then turn it a quarter turn so its
   right-hand edge points at the floor. (4 seconds)
5. **Point at the sun, upright** — Hold the phone upright, tall way up, and point the back
   camera straight at the sun. Keep the sun in the middle. (6 seconds)
6. **Sideways, top to the left** — Turn the phone sideways so the end with the camera is on
   your LEFT, and keep the sun in the middle. (6 seconds)
7. **Sideways, top to the right** — Turn the phone the other way round, so the end with the
   camera is on your RIGHT, sun still in the middle. (6 seconds)
8. **Walk with the sun** — Point the camera at the sun with the phone sideways and the
   camera end on your LEFT. Walk about 20 steps, keeping the sun in the middle. Then stand
   still for 10 seconds. (30 seconds)
9. **Tip it over the top** — Slowly tip the phone backwards, past straight up, until the
   screen faces the sky. Take about five seconds. (9 seconds)
10. **Spin it slowly** — Keep the camera pointing the same way and slowly spin the phone a
    full turn, like a steering wheel. (9 seconds)
11. **Upright, then sideways** — Hold the phone upright, then slowly turn it sideways, then
    back again. (8 seconds)
12. **Leave it alone** — Put the phone down, sideways against something, and do not touch it
    until this step finishes. (3 minutes)
13. **Walk about with it** — Pick the phone up and carry it around normally for a minute.
    Turn, walk, put it in a pocket, take it out. (1 minute)
14. **Tap the real sun** — Hold the phone sideways and point it at the sun near the LEFT of
    the screen; tap the middle of the real sun. Then turn until it is near the RIGHT and tap
    it again. (up to 1 minute)

    During this step the panel shrinks to a strip along the top edge, so the picture is clear
    for your finger. The strip says "Tap the sun halfway up near the LEFT edge, then near the
    RIGHT.", with the tap count and the seconds left on the line above. Its buttons sit at its
    right-hand end.

Notes on the awkward ones:

- **Steps 5, 6 and 7 point the camera at the sun.** Point the *phone*, and watch the screen,
  not the sky. Keep the sun's bright disc near the middle of the picture.
- **Step 8 is the one that needs room.** Twenty ordinary paces in a straight line, with the
  sun kept in the middle of the picture the whole way. Then stop and stand still for ten
  seconds without moving the phone. It is the only step that can show a compass that reads
  correctly standing still and wrongly while you walk.
- **Step 12 is the long one.** Lean the phone sideways against something solid and walk away
  for three minutes. Bumping it spoils the step.
- **Step 14 wants two taps.** Tap the sun's disc in the picture, not the label. A counter
  in the strip at the top says how many taps it has. After the second tap the strip reports
  what it measured and offers a button reading **Use this measurement** at its right-hand
  end — tap it.
- **Step 14: keep the sun halfway up the screen.** The step asks you to turn the phone so the
  sun sits near the left edge for the first tap and near the right edge for the second. Keep
  it about halfway between the top and the bottom of the picture both times. Near the top or
  the bottom corners the lens bends the picture, and the measurement comes out wrong.
- **Step 14: tap the bright disc, not a ghost.** Pointing a camera at the sun often puts a
  second, dimmer blob somewhere else on the screen, and sometimes a row of them. Those are
  reflections inside the lens. Tap the bright one you can see is the sun.
- **Step 14 measures the tilt sensor too.** Both taps together tell the app how far its own
  sense of up and down is out. So tap where the sun really is, even when the app has drawn
  its circle a long way off — the gap is the answer, not a mistake.

## Step 7 — The lining-up practice

After the fourteen steps the app runs six short attempts, headed **Lining-up practice**.

Each attempt asks you to brace the phone in both hands, pick one label on the screen, and
drag it onto the thing it names with a finger. Lift your finger when it is as close as you
can get. The first three are at normal speed. The last three are in fine mode, where the
labels move a quarter as far for the same finger movement.

How far you dragged and how steady you held the phone stay on the screen. Neither goes into
the file.

There is a **Skip the practice and finish** button. Try not to use it: this practice is the
measurement the field test's largest error term is waiting for.

## Step 8 — Email the file to yourself

The last screen says **Recording finished**, with the number of steps and the number of
sensor readings. Below it, one line should read:

> The file is complete and holds no location and no clock time (NNNNN bytes).

Then:

1. Tap **Share the file**.
2. The iPhone share sheet slides up. Tap **Mail**.
3. In the **To:** field, type your own email address.
4. Tap the blue arrow to send.

Send it to yourself and nobody else. Do not post it anywhere, do not attach it to a GitHub
issue, and do not put it in the project folder. The file records how you moved; it is yours.

If the share sheet does not appear, the app saves the file to the phone's **Files** app
instead and says so on screen. That is fine — you can email it from Files later.

## Step 9 — Tell Claude you emailed it

Claude can read that one email for you, so you do not have to copy the file anywhere.

Type this in the chat, in your own words:

> I emailed the recording to myself.

That is all Claude needs. It finds the message you just described — the recent one from you
to yourself with the recording attached — opens that one, and reads the file out of it.

**Nothing else in your mailbox is read.** Claude opens the single message you pointed it at
and no other. It does not read other threads, and it does not go looking for anything you have
not just told it about. If you would rather it did not touch your email at all, say so — you
can paste the file's text into the chat instead, or just send the lines the next section asks
for.

If Claude cannot find the message, it will ask you for the subject line or the time you sent
it. Reply with either and it will try again.

## What to tell Claude afterwards

Reply with these, in any order. Copy the sentences off the screen where you can.

- Whether the page loaded and drew labels **with airplane mode on**.
- Whether the phone **asked the three permission questions again** after the reload.
- The line beginning **"Recording finished:"** — the number of steps and sensor readings.
- The line that says whether **the file is complete** and how many bytes it is.
- Everything under **What the recording says**. That is a list of questions and answers, and
  it is the point of the session. Copy all of it.
- The two **Lining-up practice** lines at the end, one for normal and one for fine.
- Any **orange warning text** you saw at any point, word for word.
- Anything that did not happen the way this guide said it would.
- Say that you emailed the file to yourself, in those words. Keep the email: that sentence is
  what lets Claude open that one message and read the file.

## If something goes wrong

### "Turn the phone sideways"

The app draws nothing in portrait. Rotate the phone so the long edge is level. If the screen
does not rotate, swipe down from the top-right corner and switch **Rotation Lock** off — it
is the padlock with a circular arrow round it.

### "The compass needs calibrating"

Wave the phone through the air in a **figure 8**, a few times, arm out, like drawing an 8 in
the air with the phone. Then hold it up again. The message clears by itself when the phone
is satisfied.

### "The compass looks … off"

This line means the phone's compass points the wrong way by more than a nudge can fix. It
can appear during step 14. If it does, fix the direction first, then do step 14's taps.
Step 14 measures each tap against the app's own circle for the sun. With the compass far
out, that circle may not be on the screen at all, and the app then refuses the tap and
tells you to fix the direction first. Fixing the direction clears any step 14 taps made
before it, and a line under the tap count says so. Make those taps again.

1. Hold the phone still for ten seconds. Until then the button is grey.
2. Tap **Fix direction — tap the sun**. The strip at the top now reads "Fixing the direction:
   tap the picture as the line at the bottom asks."
3. Turn the phone until the real sun sits near the middle of the picture. A sun near the
   middle gives a better answer than one near the edge.
4. Tap the middle of the real sun. The labels turn, and a line says how far they turned.
5. Go back to step 14 and make its two taps, near the left and right edges as it asks.

### The camera was refused

If you tapped "Don't Allow", the app cannot ask again. Fix it in Settings:

1. Open **Settings**.
2. Tap **Apps**.
3. Tap **Safari**.
4. Tap **Camera**.
5. Choose **Allow** (or **Ask**).
6. Go back to Safari and reload the page.

(On iOS 18.2 and later, including iOS 26, Safari's own settings live under Settings → Apps →
Safari, and Camera, Microphone and Location each offer Ask, Deny or Allow. Verified against
[Apple's iPhone User Guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/browse-the-web-privately-iphb01fc3c85/ios)
and [this iOS 26 write-up](https://electronics.alibaba.com/buyingguides/safari-privacy-settings-guide-for-ios-26).)

### Your location was refused

Two switches matter, and both must be on.

1. Open **Settings**, tap **Privacy & Security**, tap **Location Services**. The switch at
   the top must be on.
2. In that same list, tap **Safari Websites**, and choose **While Using the App**.
3. Then open **Settings** → **Apps** → **Safari** → **Location**, and set it to **Ask** or
   **Allow**.
4. Go back to Safari and reload the page.

(Paths checked against
[Apple's Location Services note](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102515) and an
[iOS 26 browser-location guide](https://electronics.alibaba.com/buyingguides/how-to-enable-browser-location-on-iphone-\(ios-26\)).)

### Motion and orientation were refused

There is no switch for this one. Motion and orientation has no setting in Settings and no
entry in Safari's own per-website list, which offers Camera, Microphone and Location only.
The page asks once, Safari remembers the answer for the site, and the answer survives a
reload.

So the answer has to be cleared with what else Safari stores about the site:

1. Open **Settings**, tap **Apps**, then **Safari**, then **Advanced**, then **Website
   Data**. Find the row for `andre-sanborn.github.io`, swipe it left and tap **Delete**.
2. Open the address again and tap **Allow** when the question comes back.
3. If it does not come back, close the Safari tab completely (tap the tabs button, then the X
   on that tab) and open the address again.

**Which of those two clears it is not confirmed for iOS 26**, and the app says the same
thing on its own screen. No Apple page documents clearing this particular answer. If neither
works, tell Claude, and say which of the three permission boxes did appear.

(The removal of the old Settings → Safari → **Motion & Orientation Access** switch is
reported from iOS 13 onwards, and the per-website list's contents come from
[MacRumors on Safari's Website Settings](https://www.macrumors.com/how-to/access-website-settings-in-safari/).
Clearing the stored answer through Website Data is the remedy reported in
[this Defold forum thread](https://forum.defold.com/t/orientation-and-motion-sensors-on-ios-web-browser-safari-solved/77392).)

### Nothing loads at all

- Check the address character for character, especially `live.html?session=home`.
- Check you are not in airplane mode for the **first** load. The very first visit needs
  signal.
- Open `https://andre-sanborn.github.io/Mountain-finder/live.html` on its own. If that also
  fails, GitHub Pages is not on, and that is a job for Claude, not for you.

### The screen stays black

Wait a second — the app says "Starting the camera" while it waits for the first picture. If
it stays black, close any other app that might be using the camera, then reload the page.

## How to tell it worked

All of these, together:

- The page loaded and drew labels **with airplane mode on**.
- All fourteen steps ran, and step 14 accepted two taps.
- The last screen says the file **holds no location and no clock time**.
- **What the recording says** lists answers, and most of them are not "inconclusive".
- Six lining-up attempts were recorded, and the two summary lines both carry numbers.
- The file left the phone through **Share** and arrived in your own inbox.

If the file came out with problems listed instead, the screen says so and says how many.
Do not send it. Copy the problem lines to Claude and run the session again.
