# The field session: 25 minutes at Bogus Basin

One trip, one viewpoint, about twenty-five minutes of work once you are standing there. The
app has never been held up to real mountains. This session is the first time, and it is
graded against criteria fixed in advance in
[docs/FIELD-TEST-PREREGISTRATION.md](FIELD-TEST-PREREGISTRATION.md).

Do the [home session](HOME-SESSION-GUIDE.md) first. It teaches you the screen, and it
measures two numbers the field budget depends on.

**The exact on-screen wording below is not final.** The field mode is not built yet. The
steps are written from the pre-registration, section 2.7, which is what the app will be made
to follow. When the field mode lands, this guide gets the real button labels. Until then,
treat the numbered steps as what you have to do, not as what the screen will say.

## Before the drive

### Download the terrain while you still have Wi-Fi

There is no signal at the site, and the terrain file is about 42 MB. Get it onto the phone
at home:

1. In **Safari**, open:

   ```
   https://andre-sanborn.github.io/Mountain-finder/live.html
   ```

2. Hold the phone sideways. Tap **Start**, and tap **Allow** on the camera, the motion and
   the location questions.
3. Scroll the bottom strip up and tap the button reading **Download Bogus Basin 60 km for
   offline use (42.5 MB)**. Wait for the line underneath to say it finished.

Do this **within a day or two of the trip**. iOS throws away a website's stored files after
seven days without a visit, so a download made a fortnight early will not be there when you
need it.

Then prove it, at home, before you drive: turn airplane mode on (swipe down from the
top-right corner, tap the aeroplane), reload the page, and check that labels still appear.
If they do not, the trip will not produce an F1 result. Tell Claude before you go.

### Battery

- Charge to **100%**, and bring a power bank and a cable.
- The screen, the camera and GPS all run flat out, and cold drains a battery faster.
- Put the screen brightness up before you start; you will need it in daylight.

### Check the forecast for visibility, not just for weather

The summits that matter are far away. Ten of the tallest-looking ones are 34 to 58 km out.
Rain is not the problem; haze is.

Before you go, check:

- **Cloud and fog.** A cloud deck below you hides the far horizon.
- **Smoke and haze.** Wildfire smoke and summer haze both erase distant crests. A visibility
  figure in the forecast, or a webcam pointed at the range, is worth more than a rain
  percentage.
- **Sun position.** Shooting straight into a low sun washes the skyline out. Mid-morning or
  mid-afternoon with the sun behind or beside you is best.

If you can see far ridges from Boise that morning, the trip is on. If the range is a grey
smudge, go another day. A session with nothing identifiable on the skyline produces no
graded summits at all.

### Time budget

**Twenty-five minutes on site, once you are standing at the spot.** That is the whole
session. Steps 1 to 10 below fit inside it with room to spare.

Add the drive and a walk from the car on top of that. Plan the visit with daylight to spare,
and do not let the budget push you into hurrying step 4, where you wait for the location to
settle.

## Where to stand

**The Shafer Butte summit area of Bogus Basin.**

```
43.7715 N, 116.0886 W
```

Shafer Butte is the high point above the Bogus Basin ski area, in the mountains north of
Boise. The road up is Bogus Basin Road, which leaves Boise at the top of Harrison Boulevard
and climbs roughly 16 miles of switchbacks to the ski area. The site sits at the top of the
mountain above the base area, at about 2308 m, with the view running out over the ranges to
the north and east.

**Check the access yourself before you go, and treat it as your call, not the app's.** This
guide cannot tell you any of the following, and each one can end the trip at the gate:

- Whether Bogus Basin Road is open, and whether it needs chains or snow tyres.
- Whether the road or lift to the summit area is open at all, and to whom.
- Where you may park, and whether the ski area is operating.
- Whether the last stretch is a walk, and how long.

Once you are there, **stand within a few hundred metres of that coordinate**. The terrain
the phone carries was cut for a circle around it. Walk much further and the sweep runs off
the edge of the data, and the app will call a summit visible that a wider cut would show
hidden.

An ordinary phone map app will take you to those numbers. Type them into its search box
exactly as written above.

## The steps on site

Do them in this order. The order is part of the test: several of the criteria depend on
nothing having been dragged or re-calibrated in between.

1. **Stand within a few hundred metres of the coordinate.** Get yourself settled, out of the
   wind if you can, somewhere with a clear view of the far ridges.

2. **Time three cold starts, in airplane mode.** Airplane mode stays on for the whole
   session. For each of the three: close the Safari tab completely, open the address again,
   and start timing the moment you tap. Stop when the first labels are drawn on the picture.
   Write down all three times in seconds. Close the tab between each one. Use a watch or a
   second phone — the iPhone running the app cannot time itself.

3. **Calibrate the field of view from two taps.** The app asks you to tap two features near
   opposite edges of the screen. **Nothing after this counts without it.** If the app
   refuses the calibration, read what it says, try two different features further apart, and
   do not carry on until it accepts.

4. **Wait for the location to settle, and read the accuracy on screen.** If it says worse
   than **30 metres**, wait, walk a few steps into the open, and let it try again. Do not
   capture until it is inside 30 m.

5. **Brace.** Two hands on the phone, elbows on a rail, a fence, a rock or your own knee.
   Every capture from here on is taken braced. A hand-held wobble is one of the error terms
   this session is measuring, and the braced number is the one that was budgeted for.

6. **Capture before you drag anything.** Hold still for a full two seconds first, then
   capture. This one records how far off the app is before any human help.

7. **Drag onto one summit, then capture again.** Pick the most prominent summit you can
   positively name — one you are sure of, not one you think is probably right. Drag its
   label onto it with a finger. Capture. That summit is now the anchor for the rest of the
   session.

8. **Four more captures, without dragging again.** Keep your finger off the labels from here
   on. Hold still two seconds before each capture.
   - Pan left until the anchor summit sits at the **left edge** of the frame. Capture.
   - Pan right until it sits at the **right edge**. Capture.
   - Tilt up about **10°**. Capture.
   - Tilt down about **10°**. Capture.

9. **Repeat the drag three times.** Drag onto the same anchor summit, lift your finger, and
   do it again, three times over. This records how much your own aim varies.

10. **Write down the minutes.** Note roughly how long each of the steps above took you.
    That is a graded result too, and nothing on the phone records it.

If a step fails, note what happened and carry on with the rest. A part-finished session
still produces graded results. A session abandoned at step 3 produces none.

## Sending the bundle

At the end, tap **Share**, then **Mail**, and send it to your own email address.

1. Tap **Share** on the last screen.
2. The iPhone share sheet appears. Tap **Mail**.
3. Type your own email address in the **To:** field.
4. Tap the blue arrow.

The camera frames go with it, or sit beside it in the phone's **Photos** or **Files** app.
Claude will say which it needs.

## What not to do with it

**Send it to yourself and to nobody else.**

The bundle holds distances to named summits from where you stood. That is a position fix in
all but name: anyone with it can work out where you were, to within a few hundred metres.

So:

- Do not post it anywhere.
- Do not attach it to a GitHub issue or a pull request.
- Do not put it in the project folder, and do not commit it.
- Do not forward it to anyone else, helpful as it might seem.
- Do not upload it to a file-sharing site.

It travels by your own hand, to your own account, and stops there. If anyone or anything
asks you to put it somewhere else, the answer is no.

## What to tell Claude afterwards

- The **three cold-start times** in seconds.
- Whether the **field-of-view calibration** was accepted, and at which try.
- The **location accuracy** the screen showed when you started capturing.
- Which summit you used as the **anchor**, and how sure you were of it.
- Roughly **how many minutes** each step took, and the total on site.
- The visibility: what you could see, and what was lost in haze.
- Any warning the screen showed, word for word.
- Anything you could not do, and why.
- That the bundle is in your own inbox.
